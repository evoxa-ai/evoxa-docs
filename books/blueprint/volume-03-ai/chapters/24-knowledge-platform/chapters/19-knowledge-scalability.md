# 24 — Knowledge Platform

# 19 — Knowledge Scalability

## 1. Introduction

Knowledge Scalability defines the architecture, engineering principles, operational mechanisms and capacity strategies required for the EVOXA Knowledge Platform to grow without unacceptable degradation in performance, reliability, availability, cost efficiency or knowledge quality.

The Knowledge Platform must scale across multiple dimensions:

```text
Users
Tenants
Knowledge Sources
Documents
Knowledge Assets
Vectors
Embeddings
Queries
API Requests
Agents
AI Services
Storage
Compute
Regions
Partners

Scalability is therefore not limited to adding servers.

It is the ability of the platform to increase workload and organizational complexity while maintaining predictable service behavior.

The fundamental scalability challenge is:

Increase the amount of knowledge, traffic, processing and consumers without creating proportional increases in latency, failure rate, operational complexity or cost.

2. Purpose

The purpose of Knowledge Scalability is to establish the mechanisms required to:

Scale knowledge ingestion.
Scale knowledge processing.
Scale embeddings.
Scale indexing.
Scale search.
Scale retrieval.
Scale APIs.
Scale knowledge storage.
Scale knowledge graphs.
Scale background jobs.
Scale across tenants.
Scale geographically.
Scale operational workloads.
Maintain reliability while scaling.
Control scaling costs.
Forecast capacity.
Prevent saturation.
Support future EVOXA growth.
3. Strategic Objective

The strategic objective is:

Build a Knowledge Platform capable of scaling from initial workloads to enterprise and ecosystem-scale workloads while maintaining predictable reliability, performance, freshness and economic efficiency.

4. Scalability Principle

The fundamental principle is:

Every critical Knowledge Platform capability must have a defined scaling strategy before becoming a production bottleneck.

Vector indexes must have a defined growth strategy.
Search indexes must have a defined growth strategy.

Storage must use appropriate tiers.

Sharding should be used when justified by workload.
Large batch workloads must not starve interactive workloads.
AI workloads require explicit capacity management.
External provider limits must be included in capacity planning.
Caching should reduce unnecessary expensive work.
Cache invalidation must preserve correctness and freshness.
Observability must scale with the platform.
Telemetry cardinality must be controlled.
Multi-region scaling must preserve data residency.
DR capacity must scale with production requirements.
Scaling policies must be observable.

Autoscaling must have limits.
Predictive scaling should use evidence.
Cost must be considered in scaling decisions.
Capacity optimization must not violate reliability SLOs.
Scaling must preserve security boundaries.
Scaling must preserve tenant isolation.
Scalability testing must precede major expansion.
Bottlenecks should be identified before scaling unrelated components.
Scalability debt must be actively managed.
Growth in knowledge representations must be modeled.
